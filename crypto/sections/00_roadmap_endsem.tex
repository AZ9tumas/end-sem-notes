\section{What to Study First (End-Sem)}\label{sec:roadmap}

\subsection{What the end-sem paper looks like}
The end-sem paper of CSN15403 is \textbf{150 minutes for 50 marks}, with \textbf{six questions} to be attempted ``all, and in the same order'', and the same instruction as the mid-sem: ``precise answer with proper justification will be considered to get complete marks''. Each question opens with a paragraph of context (``Although RSA and ElGamal are secure\dots\ Justify the above statement by\dots'') and then asks for a theory part (explain the algorithm, prove its correctness, discuss its weaknesses) followed by a numerical on small numbers. A typical question is worth 5--10 marks, so about 25 minutes per question; a 10-mark numerical answer is expected to show every table (extended Euclid, square-and-multiply, point additions) and a final check. Both previous papers are solved in full in the Question Bank.

The syllabus is the whole course: the eighteen lecture decks of Dr.\ J Sathish Kumar and the official syllabus behind them. The slides are mostly Forouzan's figures and the questions follow Forouzan's (and Stallings') exercises closely, so the notes teach the textbook method wherever a slide is thin. Topics the official syllabus lists but the slides skip (modes of operation, PGP, IPSec, SSL/TLS, SET, firewalls) are in \S\ref{sec:modes} and \S\ref{sec:netsec}, and the teacher's eight assignment topics are in \S\ref{sec:assign}.

(The 2023--24 copy has only Q1--Q3 filled in, so 30 of its 50 marks are known.) Four patterns stand out. First, \textbf{a signature scheme with numbers appeared both years} (19 of the 80 known marks), always with ``explain key generation, signing, verifying'' and ``prove correctness''; Schnorr, RSA and ECDSA signatures are the ones not yet asked. Second, \textbf{a public-key numerical appeared both years} (ECC, then RSA), and the 2025--26 ElGamal question also needed primitive roots, so the number-theory toolkit is examined through these. Third, the post-mid-sem protocols (Fiat--Shamir, Diffie--Hellman) were asked as \emph{explain + small-number run + attack scenario}; Kerberos, the KDC protocols, Lamport passwords, Guillou--Quisquater and station-to-station are the natural next ones. Fourth, Lectures 10--14 (Miller--Rabin, CRT, SHA-512), which the mid-sem papers never touched, were asked in the end-sem, and the classical ciphers shrank to short notes.

\subsection{Study order}
The order puts the most marks per hour first and reuses earlier skills in later topics.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Number-theory toolkit} (\S\ref{sec:modexp}, \S\ref{sec:exteuclid}, \S\ref{sec:fermat}, \S\ref{sec:primes}). Square-and-multiply, the extended-Euclid table, Fermat/Euler exponent reduction, primitive roots, the CRT table and Miller--Rabin. Every numerical in both end-sem papers was built from these.
\item \textbf{Signatures} (\S\ref{sec:dsig}). RSA, ElGamal, Schnorr, DSS and ECDSA: key generation, signing, verifying, proof of correctness and one worked example each; then the criticism of DSS and the attacks (reused $r$, existential forgery).
\item \textbf{Public-key encryption} (\S\ref{sec:rsa}, \S\ref{sec:elgamal}). RSA with CRT decryption; ElGamal; ECC point arithmetic and ECC encryption/decryption; why ECC keys are short.
\item \textbf{Protocols} (\S\ref{sec:keymgmt}, \S\ref{sec:entityauth}). Diffie--Hellman with the man-in-the-middle scenario, station-to-station, Kerberos, Fiat--Shamir and its relatives, Lamport passwords. Practise drawing each from memory with numbered messages, and say in one line what each nonce, timestamp or signature prevents.
\item \textbf{Hashing and MACs} (\S\ref{sec:integrity}, \S\ref{sec:mac}). SHA-512 end to end, the birthday numbers, HMAC and CMAC.
\item \textbf{Block ciphers} (\S\ref{sec:modern}--\S\ref{sec:modes}). DES, double and triple DES, AES, modes of operation.
\item \textbf{Short-note topics} (\S\ref{sec:othertech}, \S\ref{sec:netsec}, \S\ref{sec:assign}, Part~I). One paragraph each for DNA and quantum cryptography, steganography/watermarking, the classical ciphers and the network-security protocols.
\item Finally, the formula sheet (\S\ref{sec:formulas}) the night before, and Mock Paper B in the Question Bank under exam conditions (150 minutes).
\end{enumerate}
